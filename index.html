EU dev pay vs cost of living

How much a software engineer keeps each month after tax, rent and everyday costs in 60 European cities, at junior, mid-level and senior pay. Click any city name (marked with an i) to see every cost and its sources.

These are estimates, not offers. Figures were compiled on 2026-11-12 from public salary platforms, salary guides, job ads, collective agreements, tax rules and rent data. Junior pay and every bonus figure rest on thin samples or assumptions. Use them to compare cities, then check real offers.

Money left each month

Each bar is take-home pay minus rent for a one-bedroom flat outside the centre, everyday costs, health insurance where it is paid separately and a monthly commute pass. The white line marks €1,500 left a month.

Typical salary vs the salary you need

Each bar is a typical posted gross salary per year. The white marker is the gross salary that leaves about €1,500 a month in that city. A bar that reaches past the marker clears it.

Where the money goes

Each bar is one month of costs split into rent, everyday costs, commute and health insurance. Costs are the same whatever you earn, so the columns on the right show what is left at junior, mid-level and senior pay. Click any city, in any chart, for its full numbers, special tax rules and every source.

Method and sources

  • Net pay is computed per country for a single employee with no children, using the latest tax year, employee social contributions and mandatory health insurance.
  • 13th and 14th salaries are included where they are standard (Austria, Portugal, Belgium, Italy). Assumed bonuses are included in take-home pay; the salary chart lets you switch them off.
  • Rent is a one-bedroom flat outside the centre. Everyday costs cover groceries, utilities, internet, phone and going out. Commute is a monthly public-transport pass for 5 working days a week.
  • Cross-border rows (Trier, Thionville, Arlon) are people working in Luxembourg and living across the border. They use Luxembourg tax, which is an assumption; check the tax treaty and telework day limits.
  • Swiss francs and other currencies are converted at the ECB reference rate of 2026-11-11. Dutch figures exclude the 27% expat tax ruling; each city's details explain the special regimes.
  • Every city lists the sources its numbers come from. Main sources: Numbeo, Levels.fyi, SwissDevJobs, StepStone, karriere.at, Austrian IT collective agreement, Morgan McKinley, PwC tax summaries and the ECB.
